- **Guard:** calibration measured 1.317 chars/token (used 1.197). 331,308 pairs, 9,939 already

### What happened

1. Batches 0-9 were created in 43 s. The 11th create returned **HTTP 429**: the Batch API caps
   requests in flight at ~100,000, and 10 × 10,000 filled it. The script crashed (a create was
   never retried, even on a 429).
2. Fixed in `792f190` (TDD, 3 tests): a 429 on create is a *rejection* — nothing is queued and
   nothing is billed — so unlike a 5xx (where the create may have landed) it is safe to retry.
   `judge_batch` now honours `retry-after`, else backs off 60 s → 10 min, up to 60 attempts.
3. The run was resumed with the fix. While it waited for quota, the 10 live batches ended — and
   61,515 of their 100,000 requests came back `invalid_request_error: "Your credit balance is too
   low to access the Anthropic API."` The balance ran out roughly 38 k requests in.
4. The run was killed at once, before it could submit the remaining 23 chunks: with no credit those
   would have produced ~230 k more error rows, and three such attempts per pair would lock those
   pairs out permanently (`MAX_ATTEMPTS = 3`).
5. The 10 batches were already paid for, so their results were banked with a new
   **`--collect-only`** mode (TDD, 2 tests): it collects every unprocessed batch, submits nothing,
   reads no pairs file, and leaves the run incomplete so the remaining 23 chunks can still be
   submitted later.
   ```
   judge_batch.py --collect-only --state data/eval/cache/universe_batch_state_full.json
   ```

## Pre-LLM prefilter (2026-09-16)

Full measurement, rejected candidates and the dropped-BUY inspection:
`.superpowers/sdd/2026-09-16-bayes-stages/prefilter-report.md`. Summary:

Toss tags several companies per article and ~74% of judged pairs score 0, so a deterministic filter
in front of the judge is worth real money. Measured against the 97,721 judged pairs in
`data/eval/cache/universe_v2.jsonl`, joined to `data/eval/universe_pairs_a6.jsonl` by key.

**Company-name matching does not work.** Dropping pairs whose company name is absent from the
article loses **24.96%** of BUYs (score > 0.15) when matching title+body and **44.48%** when
matching the title alone, even with corporate-suffix normalization, `홀딩스/지주/우/3우B` alias
peeling and a raw 6-digit-code match. Korean financial media names companies by short form
(`삼성바이오` for 삼성바이오로직스), by brand (`불닭` for 삼양식품) and by parent (`HD현대` for its
listed subsidiaries) — and the subsidiary read-through is exactly the trade. Robot/price-move title
patterns (`주가 N%`, `N% 급등`, `신고가`) cost 3.84%: they also fire on `목표주가 21% 상향` and on
`영업이익 1814% 폭증...주가 12% 폭락`, which are genuine catalysts.

**What does work** is a data-derived whitelist of syndicated roundup/table columns. Every bracketed
title prefix with n >= 30 was measured against the 17.30% base BUY rate; fourteen columns sit at
0.00-0.22% (`[표]` n=7,395 at 0.00%, `[NNNN마감체크]`, `[NNNN개장체크]`, `[개장시황]`, `[마감시황]`,
`[오늘의 IR]`, `[NNNN주요일정]`, `[오늘증시]`, `[티타임]`, `[食전食후]`, `[오늘의 증시일정]`,
`[연합뉴스 이 시각 헤드라인]`, `[AI MY 증시전망]`, `[장중시황]` and three smaller ones), while
`[모닝 리포트]` is at 48%, `[단독]` 23.8%, `[특징주]` 15.0%. `[장중수급포착]` (the robot수급 series)
is at 4.8% and is deliberately **not** filtered.

| | value |
|---|---|
| pairs skipped | 13,039 / 97,721 = **13.34%** |
| BUY recall loss | 4 / 16,908 = **0.024%** |
| `subject != mention` skipped | 0.13% |

All four dropped BUYs are post-close `마감체크` / `마감시황` wraps (three with
`reports_price_move: true`) — not tradeable that day by construction.

**Implementation.** `src/daytrade_it/application/news/mention_prefilter.py`,
`should_judge(name, code, title, body, *, enabled=None)`: pure, no I/O, and **title-only** by
design — live judging passes the full article body (~1,300 chars) while backtests pass the ~100-char
Toss summary stub, so any body-dependent rule would skip different pairs in the two worlds. One
switch (`PREFILTER_ENABLED`, `DAYTRADE_NEWS_PREFILTER`, `--prefilter`) is read by live judging
(`judge_pair`), `scripts/news_sweep_shadow.py`, `scripts/eval/build_universe_events.py` and
`scripts/eval/judge_batch.py`. **Default OFF**; behaviour with it off is unchanged. Skips are
counted and logged, and the live archive records `prefilter_skipped: true` with the matched tag so
a backtest replaying the archive sees the same universe.

**Backfill.** `data/eval/universe_pairs_a6_prefiltered.jsonl` holds the 205,163 pairs still worth
judging (233,587 unjudged minus 28,424 skipped, 12.17%). At the measured 1,127 in / 68 out tokens
and Haiku Batch pricing that is **$150.49**, down from $171.34 — a **$20.85** saving.

**Next step, not implemented.** The number of universe codes Toss tags on one article is a far
stronger signal: fan-out > 20 covers 12.4% of pairs and only 0.05% of BUYs; fan-out > 10 covers
25.8% at 0.33%. It needs the whole article→codes list at judging time, which the
`should_judge(name, code, title, body)` contract does not carry, so it needs its own design to stay
identical in live and backtest.

**Caveat for the resumable full run.** `judge_batch` applies the filter when it draws a fresh
sample, not when it resumes a stored run. The existing
`data/eval/cache/universe_batch_state_full.json` run therefore keeps its unfiltered key list; to get
the saving, start a new run against the prefiltered pairs file.

### Addendum (2026-09-16) — fan-out prefilter adopted, and a correction

**The "next step" above is now implemented and adopted.** Fan-out (how many companies Toss tags on
one article) is computable before judging in both worlds: live it is `len(article.tickers)` — the
detail call's `companyCodes` in the sweep, the feed's `relatedStocks` on the dashboard — taken
before the universe narrowing; historically it is `len(a["codes"])` from the C1 related-code
grouping. Re-measured on the same 97,721 judged pairs, **combined with the roundup tags**:

| N (drop if related codes > N) | skip % | BUY loss % |
|---|---|---|
| roundup only | 13.34% | 0.024% |
| 5 | 40.95% | 4.158% |
| 8 | 32.30% | 1.325% |
| 10 | 28.46% | 0.609% |
| **15** | **22.94%** | **0.183%** |
| 20 | 16.54% | 0.065% |

**N = 15 adopted** — the smallest tested N inside the 0.5% bar (N = 10 misses at 0.609%). The 27
non-roundup BUYs it drops are overwhelmingly `[오늘의 주요공시]` per-day disclosure lists, a roundup
column the tag whitelist does not name and this rule catches by shape; the one uncomfortable drop is
a 0.80 SK하이닉스 `[시황종합]` index wrap that reports a real +12.7% move.

The contract is now
`should_judge(name, code, title, body, related_count=None, *, enabled=None, max_codes=None)`, with
`related_count=None` (unknown) leaving the fan-out rule inert. Threshold `FANOUT_MAX = 15` /
`DAYTRADE_NEWS_PREFILTER_MAX_CODES`, read by every caller through `fanout_max()` exactly as
`prefilter_enabled()` is. `skip_reason()` returns `"roundup:<tag>"` or `"fanout:<n>"`, which is what
gets logged and written to the archive as `prefilter_reason`. `build_universe_events` now writes
`related_n` onto every pairs row so `judge_batch` applies the identical rule. Still **default OFF**;
818 unit tests pass, ruff and mypy clean.

**Backfill, regenerated:** `data/eval/universe_pairs_a6_prefiltered_v2.jsonl` on simnode — 181,633
pairs (233,587 unjudged minus 28,424 roundup minus 23,530 fan-out = 22.24% skipped), **$133.23**,
down from $171.34 unfiltered and $150.49 roundup-only. The roundup-only file is left in place.

**Correction — "live judging passes the full article body" above is wrong.** The Toss dashboard feed
carries no body (`krx_news_client`'s scraper says so explicitly), so `_parse_item` yields
`content=""` and the live daemon's v2 call passes `article.summary or article.content` = **the feed's
~100-char `summary`, or the title when it is empty**. The `full_body_chars` figure in
`data/news_judgments/*.jsonl` belongs to the **v4 shadow** scorer, which never produces a signal
(294/294 archived rows with a `full_body_chars` value also carry a `v4_shadow` block).

For the **universe pool the live sweep and the history are byte-identical**: the company-news
endpoint returns no `contentText`, so `news_sweep_shadow.build_article` reduces to `summary or
title`, which is exactly `pair_rows`' `p["summary"] or p["title"]`; the backfill copies `summary`
verbatim, and `build_prompt` renders `category or ""` so `""` (live) and `None` (history) agree.
**The 97,721 judged pairs do not need re-judging.** Measured over all 434,532 C1 rows, that
`summary` is exactly 103 chars (100 + `"..."`) in 99.76% of cases — Toss's own truncation, max 103.

Remaining asymmetry, smaller than the one it replaces: the historical fan-out count is a reverse
index over the 2,592 finished C1 codes, while live reads the article's own tag list. On the 289
archived Toss detail JSONs that are also in the C1 index, 255 (88.2%) agree exactly, 32 have live >
historical and 2 the reverse — so live skips marginally more than the measurement predicts for
articles near the threshold. Full detail in
`.superpowers/sdd/2026-09-16-bayes-stages/prefilter-report.md`, addenda A and B.
