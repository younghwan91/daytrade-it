### What happened

1. Batches 0-9 were created in 43 s. The 11th create returned **HTTP 429**: the Batch API caps
   requests in flight at ~100,000, and 10 × 10,000 filled it. The script crashed (a create was
   never retried, even on a 429).
2. Fixed in `792f190` (TDD, 3 tests): a 429 on create is a *rejection* — nothing is queued and
   nothing is billed — so unlike a 5xx (where the create may have landed) it is safe to retry.
   `judge_batch` now honours `retry-after`, else backs off 60 s → 10 min, up to 60 attempts.
3. The run was resumed with the fix. While it waited for quota, the 10 live batches ended — and
   61,515 of their 100,000 requests came back `invalid_request_error: "Your credit balance is too
   low to access the Anthropic API."` The balance ran out roughly 38 k requests in.
4. The run was killed at once, before it could submit the remaining 23 chunks: with no credit those
   would have produced ~230 k more error rows, and three such attempts per pair would lock those
   pairs out permanently (`MAX_ATTEMPTS = 3`).
5. The 10 batches were already paid for, so their results were banked with a new
   **`--collect-only`** mode (TDD, 2 tests): it collects every unprocessed batch, submits nothing,
   reads no pairs file, and leaves the run incomplete so the remaining 23 chunks can still be
   submitted later.
   ```
   judge_batch.py --collect-only --state data/eval/cache/universe_batch_state_full.json
   ```

## Pre-LLM prefilter (2026-09-16)

Full measurement, rejected candidates and the dropped-BUY inspection:
`.superpowers/sdd/2026-09-16-bayes-stages/prefilter-report.md`. Summary:

Toss tags several companies per article and ~74% of judged pairs score 0, so a deterministic filter
in front of the judge is worth real money. Measured against the 97,721 judged pairs in
`data/eval/cache/universe_v2.jsonl`, joined to `data/eval/universe_pairs_a6.jsonl` by key.

**Company-name matching does not work.** Dropping pairs whose company name is absent from the
article loses **24.96%** of BUYs (score > 0.15) when matching title+body and **44.48%** when
matching the title alone, even with corporate-suffix normalization, `홀딩스/지주/우/3우B` alias
peeling and a raw 6-digit-code match. Korean financial media names companies by short form
(`삼성바이오` for 삼성바이오로직스), by brand (`불닭` for 삼양식품) and by parent (`HD현대` for its
listed subsidiaries) — and the subsidiary read-through is exactly the trade. Robot/price-move title
patterns (`주가 N%`, `N% 급등`, `신고가`) cost 3.84%: they also fire on `목표주가 21% 상향` and on
`영업이익 1814% 폭증...주가 12% 폭락`, which are genuine catalysts.

**What does work** is a data-derived whitelist of syndicated roundup/table columns. Every bracketed
title prefix with n >= 30 was measured against the 17.30% base BUY rate; fourteen columns sit at
0.00-0.22% (`[표]` n=7,395 at 0.00%, `[NNNN마감체크]`, `[NNNN개장체크]`, `[개장시황]`, `[마감시황]`,
`[오늘의 IR]`, `[NNNN주요일정]`, `[오늘증시]`, `[티타임]`, `[食전食후]`, `[오늘의 증시일정]`,
`[연합뉴스 이 시각 헤드라인]`, `[AI MY 증시전망]`, `[장중시황]` and three smaller ones), while
`[모닝 리포트]` is at 48%, `[단독]` 23.8%, `[특징주]` 15.0%. `[장중수급포착]` (the robot수급 series)
is at 4.8% and is deliberately **not** filtered.

| | value |
|---|---|
| pairs skipped | 13,039 / 97,721 = **13.34%** |
| BUY recall loss | 4 / 16,908 = **0.024%** |
| `subject != mention` skipped | 0.13% |

All four dropped BUYs are post-close `마감체크` / `마감시황` wraps (three with
`reports_price_move: true`) — not tradeable that day by construction.

**Implementation.** `src/daytrade_it/application/news/mention_prefilter.py`,
`should_judge(name, code, title, body, *, enabled=None)`: pure, no I/O, and **title-only** by
design — live judging passes the full article body (~1,300 chars) while backtests pass the ~100-char
Toss summary stub, so any body-dependent rule would skip different pairs in the two worlds. One
switch (`PREFILTER_ENABLED`, `DAYTRADE_NEWS_PREFILTER`, `--prefilter`) is read by live judging
(`judge_pair`), `scripts/news_sweep_shadow.py`, `scripts/eval/build_universe_events.py` and
`scripts/eval/judge_batch.py`. **Default OFF**; behaviour with it off is unchanged. Skips are
counted and logged, and the live archive records `prefilter_skipped: true` with the matched tag so
a backtest replaying the archive sees the same universe.

**Backfill.** `data/eval/universe_pairs_a6_prefiltered.jsonl` holds the 205,163 pairs still worth
judging (233,587 unjudged minus 28,424 skipped, 12.17%). At the measured 1,127 in / 68 out tokens
and Haiku Batch pricing that is **$150.49**, down from $171.34 — a **$20.85** saving.

**Next step, not implemented.** The number of universe codes Toss tags on one article is a far
stronger signal: fan-out > 20 covers 12.4% of pairs and only 0.05% of BUYs; fan-out > 10 covers
25.8% at 0.33%. It needs the whole article→codes list at judging time, which the
`should_judge(name, code, title, body)` contract does not carry, so it needs its own design to stay
identical in live and backtest.

**Caveat for the resumable full run.** `judge_batch` applies the filter when it draws a fresh
sample, not when it resumes a stored run. The existing
`data/eval/cache/universe_batch_state_full.json` run therefore keeps its unfiltered key list; to get
the saving, start a new run against the prefiltered pairs file.

### Addendum (2026-09-16) — fan-out prefilter adopted, and a correction

**The "next step" above is now implemented and adopted.** Fan-out (how many companies Toss tags on
one article) is computable before judging in both worlds: live it is `len(article.tickers)` — the
detail call's `companyCodes` in the sweep, the feed's `relatedStocks` on the dashboard — taken
before the universe narrowing; historically it is `len(a["codes"])` from the C1 related-code
grouping. Re-measured on the same 97,721 judged pairs, **combined with the roundup tags**:

| N (drop if related codes > N) | skip % | BUY loss % |
|---|---|---|
| roundup only | 13.34% | 0.024% |
| 5 | 40.95% | 4.158% |
| 8 | 32.30% | 1.325% |
| 10 | 28.46% | 0.609% |
| **15** | **22.94%** | **0.183%** |
| 20 | 16.54% | 0.065% |

**N = 15 adopted** — the smallest tested N inside the 0.5% bar (N = 10 misses at 0.609%). The 27
non-roundup BUYs it drops are overwhelmingly `[오늘의 주요공시]` per-day disclosure lists, a roundup
column the tag whitelist does not name and this rule catches by shape; the one uncomfortable drop is
a 0.80 SK하이닉스 `[시황종합]` index wrap that reports a real +12.7% move.

The contract is now
`should_judge(name, code, title, body, related_count=None, *, enabled=None, max_codes=None)`, with
`related_count=None` (unknown) leaving the fan-out rule inert. Threshold `FANOUT_MAX = 15` /
`DAYTRADE_NEWS_PREFILTER_MAX_CODES`, read by every caller through `fanout_max()` exactly as
`prefilter_enabled()` is. `skip_reason()` returns `"roundup:<tag>"` or `"fanout:<n>"`, which is what
gets logged and written to the archive as `prefilter_reason`. `build_universe_events` now writes
`related_n` onto every pairs row so `judge_batch` applies the identical rule. Still **default OFF**;
818 unit tests pass, ruff and mypy clean.

**Backfill, regenerated:** `data/eval/universe_pairs_a6_prefiltered_v2.jsonl` on simnode — 181,633
pairs (233,587 unjudged minus 28,424 roundup minus 23,530 fan-out = 22.24% skipped), **$133.23**,
down from $171.34 unfiltered and $150.49 roundup-only. The roundup-only file is left in place.

**Correction — "live judging passes the full article body" above is wrong.** The Toss dashboard feed
carries no body (`krx_news_client`'s scraper says so explicitly), so `_parse_item` yields
`content=""` and the live daemon's v2 call passes `article.summary or article.content` = **the feed's
~100-char `summary`, or the title when it is empty**. The `full_body_chars` figure in
`data/news_judgments/*.jsonl` belongs to the **v4 shadow** scorer, which never produces a signal
(294/294 archived rows with a `full_body_chars` value also carry a `v4_shadow` block).

For the **universe pool the live sweep and the history are byte-identical**: the company-news
endpoint returns no `contentText`, so `news_sweep_shadow.build_article` reduces to `summary or
title`, which is exactly `pair_rows`' `p["summary"] or p["title"]`; the backfill copies `summary`
verbatim, and `build_prompt` renders `category or ""` so `""` (live) and `None` (history) agree.
**The 97,721 judged pairs do not need re-judging.** Measured over all 434,532 C1 rows, that
`summary` is exactly 103 chars (100 + `"..."`) in 99.76% of cases — Toss's own truncation, max 103.

Remaining asymmetry, smaller than the one it replaces: the historical fan-out count is a reverse
index over the 2,592 finished C1 codes, while live reads the article's own tag list. On the 289
archived Toss detail JSONs that are also in the C1 index, 255 (88.2%) agree exactly, 32 have live >
historical and 2 the reverse — so live skips marginally more than the measurement predicts for
articles near the threshold. Full detail in
`.superpowers/sdd/2026-09-16-bayes-stages/prefilter-report.md`, addenda A and B.

## Universe size — how big should the sweep universe be? top-N by prior-day traded value (2026-09-16)

Data-only. **No Anthropic call** (the credit balance is exhausted), no Toss, no Kiwoom; `kr_quant`
was read through `QuantAirflowDBClient`, whose connections are opened `readonly=True`. Run on
simnode in `~/git/daytrade-it-aplan`, branch `feat/a-plan`. The live daemon and
`/home/young/git/daytrade-it` source were not touched; the only new file is
`data/eval/universe_ranks.json`.

The question: the design fixes the sweep universe at **top 600 by the previous day's traded value**
(`application/news/universe.select_universe`, `UNIVERSE_SIZE = 600`). A smaller N costs less to
judge and, more importantly, shortens the sweep lap — how much tradeable news does it give up?

### C1. Method

`universe_ranks.json` is rebuilt exactly the way `build_universe_events.py` builds the universe:
for each of the 125 trading days, `SELECT code, trade_value FROM daily_bars_adjusted WHERE date =
<previous trading day>` → `select_universe(values, size=1000)` (deeper than 600 so every bucket edge
is checkable), rank 1 = highest traded value. Each of the 331,308 pairs in
`data/eval/universe_pairs_a6.jsonl` is then given the rank its `code` held in its `day`'s universe.
**All 331,308 pairs resolved a rank, and all 97,721 judged cache rows joined** — no drop-outs.

BUY = `v2.score > 0.15`, the same threshold used throughout this report (16,908 of 97,721 = 17.30%).

### C2. Rank buckets on the 97,721 judged pairs

| rank bucket | all pairs | judged | BUYs | BUY rate | share of all BUYs | BUYs per rank slot |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-100 | 184,739 | 54,358 | 7,993 | 14.71% | **47.28%** | 79.9 |
| 101-200 | 63,849 | 18,896 | 3,232 | 17.10% | 19.12% | 32.3 |
| 201-300 | 34,583 | 10,334 | 2,213 | 21.41% | 13.09% | 22.1 |
| 301-450 | 28,964 | 8,494 | 1,985 | 23.37% | 11.74% | 13.2 |
| 451-600 | 19,173 | 5,639 | 1,484 | **26.32%** | 8.78% | 9.9 |
| **all** | 331,308 | 97,721 | 16,908 | 17.30% | 100% | — |

Two opposing facts, and the second is the one that decides the sizing:

1. **The BUY *rate* rises monotonically down the ranking** — 14.7% at the top 100, 26.3% at 451-600.
   Mega-caps attract a huge volume of roundup, index-wrap and peer-mention articles that judge to
   0.0; a mid-cap is written about mainly when something actually happens to it. So "the tail is
   noise" is false at the per-article level.
2. **Volume swamps the rate.** A top-100 code carries ~3.4× the articles of a 451-600 code, so the
   head still produces 47% of all BUYs, and the **marginal** value of a rank slot collapses by 8×
   from head to tail: rank 50 is worth ~80 BUYs per 6 months, rank 500 ~10. Every rank slot costs
   the *same* one second of every sweep lap, so the marginal BUY-per-second-of-latency is what the
   ranking has to be cut on, and it has a knee between 200 and 300.

|---|---|---|---|---|

**Read this weakly.** Every A7-prep caveat applies: ~30% judging density, and capital (3,936

### C4. N ∈ {200, 300, 400, 600}

Judging cost basis unchanged: Haiku 4.5 Batch, 1,127 in / 68 out = **$0.0007335 per request**.
"Remaining" = rows of `data/eval/universe_pairs_a6_prefiltered_v2.jsonl` (the adopted roundup +
fan-out>15 prefilter, 181,633 rows) whose rank is ≤ N.

Sweep lap: the C2/C5 contract is 1 request/s from 08:00 to 15:20 = 26,400 requests/day, one page
request per code per lap plus one detail request per newly discovered article. Articles/day is
measured from the pairs file (distinct `news_id` whose best universe rank is ≤ N), so
`laps/day = (26,400 − articles/day) / N`.

| N | pairs/day | remaining to judge | remaining cost | articles/day | laps/day | **lap time** | **BUY coverage** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 200 | 1,989 | 131,162 | **$96.21** | 838 | 127.8 | **3.44 min** | 66.4% |
| 300 | 2,265 | 151,681 | **$111.26** | 959 | 84.8 | **5.19 min** | 79.5% |
| 400 | 2,434 | 164,655 | **$120.77** | 1,033 | 63.4 | **6.94 min** | 87.9% |
| 600 | 2,651 | 181,633 | **$133.23** | 1,135 | 42.1 | **10.45 min** | 100% |

(The N=600 lap of 10.45 min reproduces the design's "≈10 min/lap", which is a check on the model.)

**Cost is a weak lever and latency is a strong one.** Cutting 600 → 200 removes two thirds of the
universe but only 28% of the bill, because news volume is concentrated in the head that every N
keeps. The same cut takes the lap from 10.5 minutes to 3.4.

### C5. Recommendation — **N = 300**

- **Latency (the real prize).** 10.45 → 5.19 min per lap. A sweep-discovered article's expected
  detection delay is half a lap, so mean time-to-judgement drops from ~5.2 min to ~2.6 min and the
  worst case from ~10.5 to ~5.2. On intraday news that is the difference between entering on the
  first impulse and entering after it.
- **BUY coverage 79.5%** — the 20.5% given up is the part of the ranking whose marginal slot is
  worth 13 and 10 BUYs per 6 months against 22-80 in what is kept. N=200 is tempting on latency but
  gives up a third of the BUYs, and the 201-300 band is not tail noise: it is the highest-BUY-rate
  band that still has real trading volume behind it (and the best realized win rate in C3, on 39
  trades).
- **Cost $111.26** to finish the 6 months, $21.97 less than N=600. A bonus, not the argument.
- **Outcome evidence does not contradict it.** The 451-600 band bought 13 times in 100 days for
  -10,481 KRW; the 301-450 band 31 times for -8,227.

#### What changes in live

Faster detection, as above, and a lower steady-state judging rate (2,265 pairs/day vs 2,651), which
gives the single judge worker more headroom against the 08:00 overnight burst — the burst is the
one place where the queue backs up (`judge_queue` carries the backlog forward rather than pretending
judgments are instantaneous). Nothing else in the live path changes: `UNIVERSE_SIZE` is a single
constant read by both the live sweep runner and `build_universe_events`, so live and backtest stay
on one number. **Not changed by this work** — the constant is still 600; this is a recommendation.

#### What changes in the backtest

- Fewer candidates per day (2,265 vs 2,651 pairs, 959 vs 1,135 articles), so **cap competition
  changes**: the concurrent-position and deposit caps that bind today (3,936 + 2,595 skips in
  A7-prep) bind slightly later, and the first five candidates the ranker emits will be drawn from a
  head-weighted pool. Because the discarded 301-600 band has the *highest* BUY rate, the surviving
  candidate mix shifts toward lower-scoring head names unless the ranker is re-tuned.
- Any N=300 run is **a different pool** from the existing N=600 artifacts (baseline, optimizer,
  bandit). It must not be compared against them directly; pin N per experiment exactly as the
  prefilter switch is pinned.
- Re-judging is not needed: the 97,721 cached judgments are keyed on `news_id|code` and 83,588 of
  them are already inside N=300.

### C6. Budget at N = 300

| | |
|---|---|
| remaining pairs to judge (prefilter adopted, rank ≤ 300) | **151,681** |
| remaining cost, full 6 months (125 days) | **$111.26** |
| per day, of what is left to judge | $0.890 |
| per day, judging a day **from scratch** (≈77.55% prefilter survival) | $1.289 |
| **days completed per $10** (finishing days that are ~30% judged) | **11.2** |
| **days judged per $10 from scratch** | **7.8** |

For comparison at N=600: $1.066/day remaining → 9.4 days per $10; from scratch $1.508 → 6.6.

So a $10 top-up finishes about **11 whole days** at N=300 — enough for one contiguous two-week
window, which is what `judge_batch --days` (the A7-prep fix) exists to spend a budget on.

### C7. Concerns

1. **BUY is a proxy, not profit.** "Coverage" here is judged-score coverage; the A7-prep baseline
   loses money in every bucket, so 79.5% BUY coverage is 79.5% of a signal that has not yet been
   shown to be tradeable on this pool. If the edge turns out to live in the mid-cap tail, N=300 is
   the wrong cut — but nothing in C3 suggests it does.
2. **The judged set is a stratified sample over `month,sector,session`**, not over rank. Sector is
   correlated with size, so the per-bucket BUY rates carry some sampling tilt. The 29.5% density is
   fairly flat across buckets (judged/all pairs: 29.4%, 29.6%, 29.9%, 29.3%, 29.4%), which is
   reassuring but not a guarantee.
3. **The trade join inherits every A7-prep caveat** — 30% density, capital-bound replay, 100 days.
   It is directional evidence only.
4. **Rank is a prior-day quantity and it churns.** A code near the boundary moves in and out of the
   universe day to day; the buckets here are per-day ranks, not a fixed cohort, so "the 451-600
   band" is a different set of codes each day.
5. **The lap model is steady-state.** It spreads the day's detail calls evenly, while in reality the
   08:00 burst front-loads them, so the first laps of the day are longer than 5.19 min at N=300 and
   the mid-session laps shorter. The N-to-N *comparison* is unaffected.
6. **This changes no code.** `UNIVERSE_SIZE` stays 600 until someone decides; adopting N=300 also
   means regenerating the pairs file and the events, and re-running the baseline before any
   optimizer or bandit result is compared to it.

  넘겨 `judge_batch` 의 `BudgetExceeded` 가 2차 방어선 → 보고된 지출이 캡을 넘으면 다음 날을
  시작하기 전에 `BudgetOverrun`.
- **시작 전 잔액 프로브 1회**(`max_tokens=1`). 실패하면 한 날도 판정하지 않는다. 지금 막고 있는
  것이 정확히 이 400 (`credit balance is too low`) 이다.
- 선택은 **최근부터 연속된 창**(건너뛰기 없음 — LODO·리플레이가 연속 구간을 원한다). 이미 끝난
  날은 공짜로 포함.
- **99% 에 못 미친 날이 나오면 거기서 멈춘다.** 반쯤 판정된 날을 더 만드는 게 이 스크립트가
  막으려는 결과다. exit 1.
- 날마다 별도 state 파일 → 재실행은 재샘플링이 아니라 이어하기.

TDD 20개(가짜 judge 계층, API 호출 없음) + `pairs_day_summary` 7개.

**찾은 버그:** `judge_batch.cache_row_valid({})` 가 **True** 였다. `cache.get(key, {})` 로 찾은 빈
행은 `input_hash` 가 없어 legacy 분기로 떨어지고 거기서 자기 자신의 현재 입력과 같은 해시가 나온다

### H3. 밴딧 준비도 — λ 안정성·계수 부호를 fold 산출물에서 다시 쟀다

새로 판정을 사지 않고도 잴 수 있는 것은 이것뿐이라 여기만 파고들었다.
`data/eval/bandit_a7prep/lodo/folds/` 에 fold 모델 **132개**가 있고, 서로 다른 **두 실행**이
섞여 있다(A7-prep 본문은 이 중 하나만 보고했다).

| | **d40 실행** (`trained_days`=39, 40폴드, 06-10..08-14) | **d97 실행** (`trained_days`=96, 52폴드, 03-18..06-09, 미완) |
|---|---|---|
| λ | **100.0 × 40/40** | 100.0 × 45, 30.0 × 1, 10.0 × 2, 3.0 × 2, **1.0 × 2** |
| `min_prob` | 0.7 × 40 | 0.7 × 43, 0.5 × 9 |
| `min_edge` | 0.005 × 40 | 0.005 × 43, 0.0 × 9 |

λ 를 월별로 보면 d97 은 03월 {100:8, 30:1}, 04월 {100:18, 1:1, 3:1, 10:1},
05월 {100:14, 10:1, 3:1}, 06월 {100:5, 1:1} — **그리드의 양 끝(1 과 100)을 오간다.**
스펙 기준 1("시간순 폴드 사이 λ 가 한 칸 이내") **불충족**. A7-prep 본문의
"λ 100 × 40/40, 완벽히 안정"은 d40 실행만 본 결과였다.

계수 부호(모달 부호를 택한 폴드 비율):

| 특징 | d40 | d97 |
|---|---|---|
| `score` | − 100% | − 100% |
| `surprise` | + 75% | + 67% |
| `specificity` | + 100% | + 100% |
| `sentiment_strength` | **+ 98%** | **− 100%** |
| `persistence_one_off` | − 100% | − 98% |
| `persistence_multi_quarter` | + 100% | + 100% |
| `persistence_structural` | − 100% | − 100% |
| `subject_main` | − 100% | − 100% |
| `pre_entry_move` | − 98% | − 100% |
| `premarket` | + 100% | + 88% |
| `minutes_since_open` | − 100% | − 100% |
| `ret_5m` | + 100% | + 100% |
| `mkt_move` | **− 95%** | **+ 100%** |

두 가지가 드러난다.

1. **폴드 집합 *안*의 100% 는 거의 항진명제다.** LODO 폴드 40개는 학습일의 39/40 을
   공유한다 — 사실상 같은 모델 40벌이다. "폴드 간 부호 일관성"을 LODO 로 재면 안정성의
   증거가 되지 않는다. 스펙 기준 2 는 **월 단위 시간순 폴드**를 요구하는데, 지금까지의
   측정은 전부 LODO 대체물이었다.
2. **집합을 바꾸면 주요 특징 2개의 부호가 통째로 뒤집힌다.** `sentiment_strength`
   +98% ↔ −100%, `mkt_move` −95% ↔ +100%, 둘 다 자기 집합 안에서는 "완벽히 안정"이다.
   `surprise` 는 양쪽에서 불안정(75% / 67%). 부호는 풀의 성질이 아니라 **어느 날을
   봤느냐의 성질**이다. 기준 2 **불충족**.

기준 3(`bayes_judge` 채택)도 불충족이고 이건 기존 산출물 그대로다:

- 밴딧 `lodo/judge.json`: `adopt=false` — `p_mu_gt_0=0.8378 < 0.90`,
  `days_ge_0=21 < ceil(5/7*40)=29`. 다만 **0거래 기권**이라 이 숫자들은 의미가 없다.
- 옵티마이저 `opt_a7prep/stage2/judge.json`: `adopt=false` — `0.7500 < 0.90`,
  `loser_ratio_candidate=0.9091 > baseline 0.6263`, `21 < 29`.

**세 기준 모두 불충족이다.** 그동안 "1·2 는 완벽, 3 만 미달"로 읽혔던 것은 측정 방식의

| **43** | 55,019 | 16,321 | 38,698 | **$29.57** | ✓ |
| 42 | 53,642 | 15,931 | 37,711 | $28.81 | ✓ |

`spend_plan.py` 의 $29.87 과 `judge_batch` 의 $31.09 은 **모순이 아니다.** 입력 토큰 추정은

43일**을 채택한다. 실제 청구는 68토큰 쪽에 가까울 것이라 **$28.4 안팎**이 예상되고,
남는 여유는 캡을 지키는 값이다.

설계상 확인해 둔 것들:

- **`--sample 200000` 이 "온전한 날"의 장치다.** `stratified_sample` 은 `n >= len(pairs)`
  이면 전부를 돌려준다(층화가 사라진다). 43일의 판정 대상은 38,698 이므로 200k 는 곧
  "그 날들의 남은 쌍 전부". 이게 125일 30% 밀도를 만든 `--sample 200000 --stratify` 와
  다른 점은 **`--days` 로 모집단을 먼저 43일로 좁혔다**는 것 하나다.
- **`--state` 는 새 파일.** 기존 `universe_batch_state.json` / `_full.json` 은 크레딧
  사고로 끝난 층화 실행의 것이라 섞으면 안 된다. 새 파일이므로 `--new-run` 불필요.
- 모델은 기본값 그대로 Haiku 4.5 스냅샷(`judge_batch` 가 `claude-haiku-4-5` 접두를
  강제한다), 배치 50% 할인 적용된 추정치다.
- 제출이 불확실하게 실패하면(`SubmitUncertain`) **재제출하지 말고** 콘솔에서 batch id 를
  확인해 `--adopt-batch <id>` 로 붙인다. 이중 지출 방지 장치가 이것이다.

### J8. 오늘의 규칙 — 크기가 아니라 순서를 주장한다

### J9. 표본·구독·커버리지 결정은 전부 필터다 — 상시 규칙

> **전략이 결정 시점에 갖지 못했을 정보로 내린 표본·구독·커버리지 결정은 전부 필터이고,
> 작성 당시 필터라고 불리지 않았더라도 필터로서 감사해야 한다.**

오늘 하루에 이 모양이 넷 나왔다: scalp-it 의 `len(ticks)>=500` 과 구독 종목 집합,
`edge_livepool` 의 가격 있는 티커 집합 누수, 그리고 여기 **(a) 코호트** —
`--stratify month,sector,session` 로 뽑은 ~62일. 섹터와 세션은 결과와 상관이 있고
`persistence` 는 섹터와 거의 같은 말("structural" 대 "one_off")이므로, 그 62일의 **날 안**
버킷 구성은 표본 설계 자체에 의해 왜곡돼 있다.

**결정(구속력 있음): (a) 코호트는 `persistence` 날내 대조의 1차 분석에서 제외하고, 별도
층으로 부호만 확인한다.** 부호가 다르면 정보이고, 같으면 약한 방증일 뿐 병합하지 않는다.
판정 캐시 156,605건은 **세 가지 설계**가 섞여 있다: (a) 층화 ~62일, (b) 인구조사에 가까운
완료 63일, (c) 앞으로 살 날내 균등 200/일. **(b)와 (c)는 날내 대조에서 깨끗이 합쳐지지만
(a)는 아니다.**

### J14. 넓게 받고 좁게 산다 — 백필 상시 규칙

버리고 얻는 게 없다.** 수집 범위와 판정 범위는 별개 결정이다 — 42개월 아카이브에서 2023-09
이후만 표본해 그만큼만 지불하면 된다.

**공매도 금지 구간이 이 규칙의 값을 보여준다.** 재개 2023-11-06, 해제 2025-03-31. 거래일 기준:

| 범위 | 금지 전 | 금지 중 | 금지 후 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24개월 (2024-09) | **0** | 132 | 365 |
| 36개월 (2023-09) | **33** | 348 | 365 |
| 42개월 (2023-03) | **159** | 348 | 365 |

36개월도 세 체제를 걸치긴 하지만 금지-전 구간이 33거래일뿐이다. 42개월은 **4.8배**이고,
추가 6개월이 사 주는 것은 정확히 그 팔 하나다. 다른 관측이 전혀 없는 체제라 거기가 희소하다.

### J16. 어느 스냅샷에서 계산했는가 — 오늘 네 번째로 문제가 된 것

코호트 분할 1차 시도에서 **인구조사 날 하나가 24.8% 로 읽혔다.** 실제로는 99% 판정된 날인데,
분할을 두 번째 트랜치가 착지하기 **전에** 만들어진 요약 CSV 로 계산했기 때문이다. 그대로
보냈으면 깨끗한 63일 중 하나가 오염된 날로 격리됐을 것이다.

그래서 `scripts/eval/cohort_split.py`(신규)는 **캐시와 페어 파일에서 커버리지를 매번 다시
계산한다** — 요약 CSV 를 읽지 않는다. 그리고 두 코호트가 평소 남기는 빈 띠(인구조사 0.98+,
층화 0.32-)가 좁아지면 경고하고 비정상 종료한다. 그 띠가 닫히는 것이 바로 이 오류의 모양이기
때문이다. 테스트 5개.

현재 값(실시간 계산): **인구조사 63일 (최소 0.9830), 층화 62일 (최대 0.3193)** —
`data/eval/cohort_split.json`. 두 집단이 0.66 의 빈 띠를 사이에 두고 갈려서, 구매 이력을
재구성하지 않고 **커버리지만으로 검증 가능**하다.

이 문서에서 2차 트랜치 착지 이전에 계산된 커버리지 숫자는 43일 트랜치에 대해서는 맞지만
t63 20일을 과소평가한다. J2 의 값은 재생성한 원장 기준이다.

### J17. J10 정정 — 24개월 $55 가 아니라 42개월 $109, 그리고 그 돈이 못 사는 것

36개월이 아니라 42개월인 이유는 J14 의 공매도 표다 — 36개월의 금지-전 구간은 33거래일로,

것이 있을 때만 값을 한다.
